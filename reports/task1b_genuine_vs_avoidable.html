Task 1 · Part B — Rate & Drivers

Genuine vs. Avoidable

7.93% of users are not verified — 2.93 points over the brief's 5% bar, or 73,663 people. Half of that gap traces to one routing defect. The rest splits cleanly into what a fix can reach and what it can't: 75.6% of every non-verification is process failure, avoidable in principle; only 24.4% looks like the system correctly saying no.

This document answers the brief's second ask, in order: measure the rate against the 5% bar, then diagnose the drivers — separating genuine rejections from avoidable losses and quantifying the largest of each.

Analysis denominator 2,515,155 kyc.kyc_users, test accounts excluded (107 flagged Test_user rows, never deleted). Used for every figure below unless noted otherwise.

Diagnose the drivers

One routing gap is half the problem

Not that users fail checks. It's that 101,162 users failed the primary check (Idology) and then had no second check run at all — never routed onward into the waterfall. That's 50.7% of every non-verification in the dataset, from a single defect.

Stranded, no 2nd check 101,162 101,159 not verified · 3 verified anyway (data quirk)
Recovery when routed onward 48.09% same starting point — failed Idology first
Projected rate if fixed ~6.0% from 7.93%, routing alone, no control loosened
Evidence this is a routing failure, not a harder population. Users who failed Idology and were routed onward recovered at 48.09%. The stranding rate is also flat at ~35% across every partner and every enrolment year — consistent with the stranded group not being a specially hard segment, though not a direct test of verification difficulty (no secondary check ever ran on them to measure that against), so this is supporting evidence, not proof. Applying the 48.09% rate to the stranded population projects ~48,649 recoverable users — roughly two-thirds of the gap to the 5% bar. Stated plainly where it was stated in the notebook: this is an estimate from a comparable group, not a guarantee — the honest way to confirm it is to route a sample and measure.
Genuine vs. avoidable

Four ways a user goes unverified

The distinction the brief asks for: a genuine rejection is a user properly assessed whom the system was right to decline. An avoidable loss is a user who could plausibly have verified, lost to process rather than to risk. The rule applied: a user assessed by two or more checks and failed by all of them is a genuine rejection; a user who failed one check and was never given another is an avoidable loss — the system never finished asking the question.

Bucket D — assessed by two or more providers and a human, and still declined. 24.4% of every non-verification. This is the number this analysis is not trying to shrink — no evidence it's addressable without weakening a control.

Worth flagging

Small, checked, and on the record

None of these move the headline numbers above. Each surfaced while verifying this page and was chased down rather than left as a loose end. Full breakdowns and queries: TASK1_REPORT_DRAFT.md.

1

Bucket B isn't 100% “failed Idology”

101,159 of 101,325 are the core finding. The other 166: 146 PASSed Idology with nothing else run and still aren't verified; 18 PASSed a direct Persona-IDV check — a third undocumented entry point, Idology never touching them at all; 2 FAILed via the LexisNexis door. All three shapes contradict “never routed on,” since a check did run.

2

Bucket C has the same anomaly, larger

1,131 of 48,598 (2.33%) have at least one provider PASS yet remain unverified with no manual review. Persona-IDV-after-Idology-FAIL and Idology-PASS-then-ACRO-FAIL convert at only 83–86% dataset-wide — meaningfully weaker than the >99% seen elsewhere, not noise. Uniform across partners (1.78–1.94% of each), so not one bad integration.

3

That pattern totals 2,093 across the whole dataset

164 (bucket B) + 1,131 (bucket C) + 798 (bucket D, where manual review did run — a human overriding a PASS, plausibly intended). The 1,295 with no human involved at all (0.65% of all non-verification) is the part worth treating as a real, if small, defect.

4

kyc_source needed auditing, not trusting

It names one credited clearer per user, not every check that passed — confirmed by cross-checking every row against the result columns directly. Found: 2 outright contradictions (a provider credited despite every column, including manual review, saying FAIL); 17 users verified with zero PASS anywhere; and 85,172 cases of multi-check overlap that turned out to be expected (e.g. Idology and LexisNexis both PASS, credited to Idology alone) — not a bug. Every check-level figure on this page is built from the result columns, never from kyc_source.

Caveats

What could make these numbers wrong, and how it was handled

1

Test accounts

107 internal Test_user QA fixtures are excluded from every figure — flagged via is_test_user, never deleted. Barely moves anything at this scale, but they aren't real applicants.

2

The 48,649 “recoverable” figure is a projection, not a measurement

Built from an observed comparison group's recovery rate — stated as an estimate, not a guarantee. The honest way to confirm it is to route a sample and measure.

3

The reviewer-comment reason taxonomy has limited coverage

Comments exist for ~3% of the book, skewed toward manual review — they describe the reviewed population, not the whole book, and have zero coverage of the stranded population, since nothing ever looked at them.

Denominators used on this page. The book is 2,515,155 users (2,515,262 less 107 flagged test accounts). Non-verification figures use that same denominator throughout.
