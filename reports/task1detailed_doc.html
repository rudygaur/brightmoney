Data caveats, and how each was handled

1

The denominator that would have made this report say 0.09%

kyc_source is null for 197,314 users and 99.99% of them are unverified. It looks like missing data; dropping those rows gives a non-verification rate of 0.09% instead of 7.93%. It is not missing data — the field names the provider that cleared a user, so it is null precisely when nobody did. Proof: not one of those rows carries a PASS on any provider. Excluding them would exclude almost every rejection by construction. Kept in, and this is the single most important denominator decision on the page.

2

2,122 users are not verified despite passing something

Including 393 who passed Idology and 34 who passed manual review. These contradict the documented design. They are flagged, not corrected — overriding overall_kyc_status would be editorialising the outcome field the whole analysis is measured against. At 1.06% of non-verifications they move the headline by under 0.1 points either way.

3

Reviewer comments cover 3% of users and cannot predict outcomes

Free text exists for the manually reviewed population only, so the reason taxonomy describes reviewed users, not all failures — the stranded cohort has zero comments. And every one of the 1,889 distinct comment strings maps to exactly one manual decision, so the comment is written with the decision. It can describe composition; it cannot be used as an independent predictor.

4

No cost or latency data exists in this extract

There are no per-check timestamps and no provider pricing, so “cheaper” and “faster” claims here rest on the ordinary economics of these check types, not on measurements from this dataset. Task 2's trade-offs use illustrative unit costs against real volumes and say so; collecting the real numbers is a Task 3 item.

Diagnose the drivers

Genuine rejection, or avoidable loss?

Both look identical in the outcome field. They separate on how much assessment the user actually received before the system said no — which is recorded, check by check, for every row.

Avoidable loss

The waterfall never finished. One check ran, or none — no second provider, no human. The question was closed before it was asked.

Genuine rejection

Two or more providers and a human analyst assessed the user, and all of them declined. The system working as designed.

What it adds up to

Two fixes close most of the gap

Neither weakens a control, moves a threshold, or lets through anybody who should be rejected. Both cost provider calls and analyst capacity, not risk appetite.

Today — 199,421 users not verified 7.93%
Route every Idology failure onward. Applies the observed 48.09% recovery rate to the 101,159 stranded users. −48,6475.99%
Triage the 2+-failure cases to review. 53,312 users who failed two or more automated checks never reached a human. −3,9455.84%
Remaining gap to the 5% bar — 21,071 users 0.84 pp5.00%

Routing alone moves 7.93% to roughly 6.0% — two-thirds of the gap, nothing loosened. The second fix is sized on the incremental 7.40-point lift (17.84% verify with review, 10.44% without), not review's headline 32.30% clear rate — crediting that would double-count users who already verify by other means. The last 0.84 points has to come from the redesign in Task 2.

Reading this responsibly
01

Both recovery figures are projections, not experiments

They assume stranded users behave like routed users who failed the same check — defensible because stranding is uniform across every partner and year, but not a direct test: no secondary check ever ran on the stranded group, so there is nothing to measure against. The honest confirmation is to route a sample and measure. Sizing, not a commitment.

02

2,093 users are not verified despite passing a check

164 in bucket B, 1,131 in C, 798 in D. The 798 had a human review them, so an override is plausibly intended; the other 1,295 — 0.65% of non-verification, no human involved — are a real if small defect. Flagged, not corrected: overriding overall_kyc_status would edit the outcome field the analysis is measured against. Moves the headline under 0.1 points either way.

03

Reviewer comments cover 2.96% of the book and cannot predict outcomes

Free text exists for the manually reviewed population only — it fully characterises bucket D but has zero coverage of the stranded population, since nothing ever looked at them. Every distinct comment maps to one manual decision, so it is written with the decision: it describes composition, it does not predict.

04

kyc_source was audited, not trusted

It names one credited clearing provider per user, not every check that ran or passed — so every check-level figure here comes from the individual result columns instead.
