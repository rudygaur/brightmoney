The one number I would not defend as measured — and what happens if it is wrong

Changes 1–5 are grounded in observed comparison groups and land at 5.69%. Change 6 is the residual: it is calibrated to close the remaining gap, not derived independently, and saying otherwise would be dressing an assumption as a finding. What it assumes is that Persona IDV converts 18.8% of the ~92,000 users who would reach it. Today it converts 86.32% — but on a hand-picked cohort of 2,507 people someone already expected to finish a document upload, so that figure is selection-biased upward and 18.8% is the discounted ask, against a 30.72% baseline for Idology failures who never see it.

Even so, the bar moves if the discount is wrong. At a 10% conversion the redesign lands at 5.32%, not 5.00% — still a 2.6 point improvement, still short of the brief. That is the first thing a phased rollout should measure.

The full mechanism Page 2 / 5 — the waterfall diagram

How a user moves through the redesigned waterfall

Read top to bottom. Rectangles are automated checks; diamonds are decisions. Blue = pass, verified. Red = a sanctions/PEP/OFAC flag — hard-routed to a human immediately, at every check, not just the first. Amber = manual/compliance review, the terminal stop for every path.

Every arrow ends somewhere defined. That is the entire redesign — nothing here is a new check; it’s a guarantee that existing checks are always reached, and that a sanctions/PEP/OFAC hit at any stage — not only Idology — skips every remaining automated fallback and goes straight to a human.

Routing logic, in priority order

  1. Idology runs on every applicant. No bypass, no alternative primary, no exceptions.
  2. A sanctions/PEP flag goes straight to compliance — regardless of the identity match result, skipping every automated fallback. This is the one route that gets less automation, not more, and it is a deliberate strengthening: today the signal is folded into a generic FAIL, so a stranded user’s watchlist status is not merely unresolved, it is unobserved.
  3. A clean Idology PASS verifies the user and ends the flow. Unconditionally. Nothing downstream, automated or human, may reopen it.
  4. An Idology FAIL is classified, and the class sets the path.
    • SSN_MISMATCH → legacy accounts to Persona-SSN, standard accounts to LexisNexis; exactly one runs. PASS verifies. On FAIL, apply the brief’s crucial rule: if the result surfaces a secondary non-SSN issue, cross to the non-SSN track; otherwise go to L4. There is no second SSN check to fall back to, because the two are alternatives.
    • IDENTITY_ATTR_MISMATCH → ACRO, then on failure Persona IDV, then on failure L4.
    • NO_DATA → the applicable SSN check and ACRO run in parallel; any PASS verifies. If both fail, one Persona IDV attempt, then L4. Parallel rather than sequential because the classifier has no basis for choosing a track, and running them in series would double the latency for a case that is already the least informative.
  5. Manual review is the last stop on every path. PASS verifies; FAIL is a hard reject.

Stop conditions — where a user is hard-rejected, and why

ConditionWhy it is final
Confirmed sanctions / PEP / OFAC match, upheld by compliance review A legal decline, not a conversion problem, and never resolvable by routing to a friendlier check. The control is noisy — of 9,994 flagged cases today, 78.7% are cleared as false positives — which is precisely why the human, not the automation, holds the authority to uphold it.
SSN mismatch upheld by manual review The largest named rejection reason in the reviewer comments, and deliberately not a target. Manual review upholds it in ~99.9% of cases. Chasing this category means weakening SSN verification to buy a few hundred users — the wrong trade in a US KYC programme.
Every automated check on the assigned path exhausted, and manual review declines The genuine-rejection floor: 48,655 users today, already assessed by two or more providers and a human. This design does not try to shrink it, because Task 1 found no evidence it is addressable without weakening a control.

Two things that are deliberately not stop conditions

An automated FAIL, on its own, at any tier. No user in this design receives a final no without a human seeing the case. That is a strengthening of the control posture, not a loosening of it — it is also what drives the manual-review cost on page 4.

And symmetrically: a clean PASS is as final as a hard reject. Today 1,295 users hold a provider PASS, were never reviewed by anyone, and are declined anyway. A rule that only binds in the rejecting direction is not a rule.

Adding checks is not free Page 4 / 5 — cost, latency, friction

What this costs, and who pays it

In one sentence: this design buys ~21,600 verified users a year by asking human reviewers to do roughly 2.25× the work, and by putting tens of thousands of people through a document-and-selfie flow that almost nobody sees today. Three cost effects, sized against real volumes

1 · More automated calls
+$52–78K/yr

Routing 101,162 stranded users through 1–2 fallbacks each, plus one automated attempt for the 24,779 sent straight to a human — roughly 52,000 extra provider calls a year. The cheapest of the three effects, and it buys the single largest recovery.

2 · Persona IDV volume
+$92K/yr

From 761 cases a year to roughly 27,000 — a 36× increase. This is the friction cost as much as the money: a 60–120s document flow, at that volume, is a real drop-off risk that verification data alone cannot measure. It sits last in the path for exactly this reason.

3 · Manual review — the real trade
+$219K/yr

From ~21,800 cases a year to ~49,200 — 2.25×, or about 7.3 additional analyst FTEs. This is the largest number in the design and the one most worth challenging with real ops data before anyone commits to a headcount plan.

Latency and friction, honestly

Best case
Unchanged. 88.10% of applicants clear Idology in under a second and exit — and under this design they exit faster, because nothing is permitted to run after a clean PASS. Today 130,713 passing users pick up a redundant provider call.
Worst case
Longer, and deliberately so: L1 → L2 → 60–120s of document capture → hours or days at L4. Set against the alternative, which is what 101,162 people get today — no second check, no human, and no answer at all.
Sanctions cases
Faster at zero extra cost. All 9,994 flagged cases already reach a human eventually; routing them there immediately rather than after automated fallbacks removes wait, adds no volume.
Where friction
is avoided
ACRO runs before Persona IDV and clears 44.84% of what it sees. Every user resolved at that cheap, invisible step never sees a document upload — which is the whole argument for the ordering.

Preserved, changed, removed

Preserved
Idology as the universal L1. Manual review as final decision authority. The brief’s four-tier structure and its standard-vs-legacy SSN split. The SSN→non-SSN crossover. And the genuine-rejection floor — 48,655 users, ~24% of non-verification, deliberately not targeted.
Changed
Sanctions/PEP becomes an independently-routed flag rather than a signal folded into ordinary failures. Manual review becomes a guarantee. LexisNexis unifies into one secondary role. Persona IDV becomes a real fallback instead of a rarity.
Removed
The LexisNexis primary-position side door. The possibility of any check running after a clean PASS. The dead end where a failure leads nowhere.

What would make this wrong — in the order I would go check

RiskWhy it matters, and what I would do about it
The L1.5 classifier may not be buildable as specified The entire design assumes Idology’s response distinguishes an SSN mismatch from an identity-attribute mismatch from a sanctions hit — and that an SSN-path failure can surface a secondary non-SSN signal. That is an assumption about a vendor API, not a finding from this data. Confirm what the actual API responses contain before anything else is built. If they do not, the classifier needs a rules layer over the raw response codes, and that is a materially larger build.
Persona IDV may not convert at 18.8% The load-bearing assumption on page 1. At 10% the design lands at 5.32% and misses the bar. Measure it first, on a routed sample, before sizing the vendor contract.
2.25× manual review is a hiring plan, not a config change ~7.3 additional FTEs cannot be stood up on day one. This needs a phased rollout with a triage rule that prioritises cases review can actually resolve — and the natural follow-on is a risk-based auto-decline layer, which needs labelled outcome data this system will only start generating once it is live.
“Legacy account” has no definition The L2a/L2b gate is preserved from the brief, but no signal in this data — age, vintage, or partner — reproduces which accounts qualify. It needs a real condition from account services before build, not a proxy invented to make the diagram close.
