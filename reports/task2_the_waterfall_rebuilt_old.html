The Waterfall, Rebuilt

Task 1 found that half of every non-verification was a single failure to route users onward, not a genuine decline. This redesign makes that failure mode structurally impossible — every check outcome gets a defined next step, and nobody exits without either verifying or being seen by a human.

Before the fix

What the current waterfall actually does — audited, not assumed

A redesign is only as credible as the audit of what it replaces. Every distinct path taken through today’s system was enumerated — all 38 of them, no volume cutoff — and checked against the brief’s literal rules. The one-sentence fix

Today, a failure can be a dead end. In the redesign, it can’t.

Everything else in this document is detail. This is the mechanism the detail supports.

Today

101,162 people failed the first check and got no second one, no human review — and no record of why. 50.7% of every non-verification in the dataset.

Redesign

Every failure gets a reason class and a defined fallback. Nobody exits the waterfall without either verifying or reaching a human — the full routing tree is below.

The full mechanism

How a user moves through the redesigned waterfall

Read top to bottom. Blue = the automated path, still fast and free to the user. Red = sanctions/PEP — hard-routed to a human immediately, never resolved by another automated check. Amber = manual/compliance review, the terminal stop for every path.

Every arrow ends somewhere defined. That is the entire redesign — nothing here is a new check; it’s a guarantee that existing checks are always reached.

Where a user is finally declined

Two stop conditions. Only two.

The brief asks where a user is hard-rejected, and why. Everything else in this design is a fallback, not an ending.

STOP 1

Confirmed sanctions / PEP / OFAC match

After compliance review. Never resolved by routing to a friendlier automated check — a true match is a legal decline, not a conversion problem. This is the brief’s non-negotiable, written as a rule instead of left to emerge from sequencing.

STOP 2

Exhausted every automated fallback, then failed manual review

The genuine-rejection floor. Task 1 sized this population at 48,655 users (24.4% of all non-verifications) already assessed by 2+ providers and a human — not targeted for reduction, because Task 1 found no evidence it’s addressable without weakening a control.

Not a stop condition, deliberately: an automated FAIL alone, at any tier. Every path in this design reaches a human before a final no — a strengthening of today’s control posture, not just a conversion fix (see the sanctions finding below).
A rule that isn’t a stop condition, but is just as non-negotiable: a clean Idology PASS also ends the flow, unconditionally, in the other direction. The audit above found 131,538 users where it doesn’t today, including 201 who passed and still weren’t verified. Under this design a PASS is as final as a hard reject — nothing downstream may re-open it.
New evidence

The redesign closes a blind spot the current design can’t even measure

Found while checking whether “route everyone” could touch sanctions handling before proposing it as the headline fix — not in the original Task 1 pass.

9,994
users with a sanctions / watchlist / PEP / OFAC mention in review notes
100%
of those 9,994 sit inside an Idology FAIL — the same undifferentiated signal as an ordinary mismatch
0
of the 101,162 stranded users have any reviewer comment — no signal was ever recorded

The honest claim here is narrower than it might sound, and still consequential. It is not “N sanctions hits are being missed” — that’s unprovable, since a stranded user’s true status is unobserved by definition. It is: today’s design has zero visibility into this population’s risk profile, because sanctions signal and generic identity-mismatch signal are folded into one undifferentiated FAIL, and the stranded state skips every point — a secondary check, a human reviewer — where that signal could otherwise surface.

Closing “stranded” closes that visibility gap as a side effect. Decoupling the sanctions flag into its own always-checked signal (L1.5) is what makes closing it a hard rule instead of a lucky accident of sequencing — which is why this redesign is risk-positive, not merely risk-neutral.

Every change, tied to Task 1

Nothing here is invented

1
Task 1 finding

101,162 users failed Idology and were never routed onward — 50.7% of every non-verification, the single highest-leverage finding.

Redesign response

The reason classifier (L1.5) and the routing tree guarantee every failure gets a path. “Stranded” becomes structurally impossible.

2
Task 1 finding

Manual review reached only 30.2% of users eligible for it — a hole exactly where the design says it should catch people.

Redesign response

Every path now terminates at manual review if automation doesn’t resolve it. Coverage goes from “sometimes” to “always.”

3
Task 1 finding

Persona IDV clears 86.3% of what it’s tried on vs 30.7% without it — but ran on under 1% of Idology failures. The best tool, barely used.

Redesign response

Promoted to the standard second non-SSN fallback (L3) — kept after the cheaper ACRO check, since it’s also the highest-cost, highest-friction step.

4
Task 1 finding

The “older accounts → legacy Persona-SSN” rule showed no age or vintage signal anywhere in the data — not reproducible, reported honestly rather than guessed.

Redesign response

Restored, not retired. The brief specifies legacy accounts route to Persona-SSN and standard accounts to LexisNexis as alternatives — that split is kept by decision. Flagged honestly: since no reproducible signal identifies “legacy” in this dataset, the operational definition is an open dependency for Task 3, not invented here.

5
Task 1 finding

94,183 users (3.7%) never saw Idology — not one undocumented entry point but three: LexisNexis (93,322, mostly ProviderA_Lexis_Nexis), no check at all (843), and a direct-to-Persona-IDV path (18). A control question, not just routing.

Redesign response

Idology becomes the sole, mandatory L1. ProviderA/B are unified into one documented secondary role, reached only after a real primary check.

6
New evidence

Sanctions/PEP screening rides entirely on the same undifferentiated Idology FAIL as ordinary mismatches, and zero stranded users have any record of why they failed.

Redesign response

Sanctions/PEP becomes its own always-checked flag with a hard, immediate route to compliance — skipping automated fallbacks rather than waiting for them to fail first.

7
New evidence

The flow audit above found 131,538 users get a clean Idology PASS and the waterfall runs further checks anyway — 201 of them end up not verified despite passing, contradicting the brief outright.

Redesign response

PASS becomes an unconditional stop, symmetric with the hard-reject stop conditions. No downstream check may run once Idology returns a clean PASS.

8
Self-identified gap

The brief's “crucial rule” — an SSN-path check surfacing a secondary non-SSN issue moves the user to the non-SSN track — is already real and working today: 8,583 users (0.34%) hit it, recovering at 54.9%. This design's own first draft never actually drew that rule into the routing tree.

Redesign response

Added explicitly: on an SSN-path FAIL, the classifier re-checks for a non-SSN signal before falling to manual review. A positive signal crosses the user to ACRO → Persona IDV instead. See the routing tree above.

Adding checks is not free

Where the cost of this redesign actually goes

No provider unit costs or latencies exist in this dataset. The figures below apply clearly-labelled illustrative unit costs to the real Task 1 volumes — the volumes are load-bearing, the unit costs are not.

Effect 1 · automated calls
+$120K–240K/yr

Routing the 101,162 stranded users through 1–2 automated fallbacks each — ~100,000–160,000 additional provider calls, in Task 1’s observed 57/29/13 branch split.

Illustrative: blended $1.00–1.50/call. Not yet re-derived for the legacy gate / crossover below — hover for why.
Effect 2 · manual review, the big one
+$238K/yr · ~8 FTE

Bucket C (48,598, already exhausted automation) plus the ~52,552 newly-routed stranded who still fail automation — roughly a 2.4× increase in review volume, ~29,750 more cases/year.

Illustrative: $8/case, 15 cases/analyst/day
Effect 3 · Persona IDV friction
10–20× volume

From ~763/yr today to tens of thousands — the most expensive automated check ($3.50) and by far the highest-friction one: 60–120s of document capture and a selfie.

Real drop-off risk, not quantifiable from this dataset
This design recovers most of the gap to 5% largely by asking human reviewers to do roughly 2.4× today’s volume — a real staffing decision, not a free routing fix. It should be sized with actual ops numbers before anyone commits to a headcount plan.
Summary

Preserved, changed, removed

Preserved

  • Idology as the universal L1
  • Manual review as final decision authority
  • The brief’s four-tier L1→L2/L3→L4 structure
  • The brief’s standard-vs-legacy SSN split (LexisNexis or Persona-SSN, as alternatives) — kept by decision, though this dataset can’t reproduce which accounts count as “legacy”
  • The brief’s SSN→non-SSN “crucial rule” crossover — now explicit instead of an accident of which checks fired
  • The genuine-rejection floor (~24.4%) — not targeted, no evidence it’s addressable without weakening a control

Changed

  • LexisNexis unified into one documented SSN-path role
  • Sanctions/PEP becomes an always-checked, independently-routed flag
  • Manual review triage becomes a guarantee, not a possibility
  • The non-SSN double-check cascade (ACRO then Persona IDV) becomes official sequential policy

Removed

  • The 3.7% Idology-skip entry points — three of them: LexisNexis (mostly ProviderA) acting as an undocumented primary, no check at all, and a direct-to-Persona-IDV path
  • The possibility of a check running after a clean PASS
  • The SSN-side double-check (LexisNexis and Persona-SSN on the same person) — the legacy/standard split makes this structurally impossible, correcting this document’s own earlier draft
Handing this to engineering

Open items for Task 3

1

The reason classifier (L1.5) is the load-bearing dependency. This design assumes Idology’s response carries, or can be made to carry, structured fields distinguishing an SSN mismatch from an identity-attribute mismatch from a sanctions hit — plus, for the crucial-rule crossover, a signal for when an SSN-path check surfaces a secondary non-SSN issue. Confirming what the actual API responses contain is the first build item.

2

“Legacy account” needs an operational definition. The redesign restores the brief’s standard-vs-legacy SSN split, but Task 1 found no signal in this dataset (age, enrollment date, bank partner) that reproduces which accounts qualify. Before build, confirm with account services or engineering what actually defines “legacy” so L2b’s gate has a real condition to evaluate.

3

The ~2.4× manual review increase is the biggest open risk. Sized from real Task 1 proportions but illustrative unit costs — the staffing plan needs real numbers and probably a phased rollout rather than 100% of traffic on day one.

4

A risk-based auto-decline layer could meaningfully cut the manual review increase — not designed here, since it needs labelled outcome data this dataset doesn’t provide, but worth flagging as the natural next optimisation once this design is live and generating its own data.

5

Persona IDV’s friction cost isn’t just financial. A 10–20× volume increase into a document-and-selfie flow is a real drop-off risk this dataset can’t quantify — it needs product funnel telemetry, not verification-system data. Worth an explicit phased rollout rather than a flip of the switch.

Reading this responsibly

Assumptions & data caveats

Every judgment call behind this design, stated plainly — so any of them can be defended, or revised, under questioning.

1

This design inherits Task 1’s denominators — and its test-account exclusion

The 199,421 non-verified and its 101,162-user stranded population already exclude the 107 internal Test_user accounts (flagged via is_test_user, never deleted, filtered with where not is_test_user in every query on this page), and still include the 843 real users who never had any check run — the same scope decisions carried over from Where the Funnel Breaks. See that report’s caveats for the full reasoning.

2

The sanctions/PEP claim is narrower than it might sound

The finding above is not “N sanctions hits are being missed” — that’s unprovable, since a stranded user’s true status is unobserved by definition. It is that today’s design has zero visibility into that population’s risk profile. Don’t overstate it as a discovered loss; it’s a discovered blind spot.

3

The recovery and review-volume estimates are projections, not results

Both the ~48,649-person recovery figure (7.93% → ~6.0%) and the ~2.4× manual review increase are comparison-group / proportional projections from Task 1’s observed rates, not a controlled experiment or a pilot. Carry that caveat every time either number is quoted, not just here.

4

The reason classifier (L1.5) is an assumption, not a confirmed capability

The whole routing tree depends on Idology’s response carrying, or being made to carry, structured fields that distinguish an SSN mismatch from an identity-attribute mismatch from a sanctions hit. This dataset can’t confirm that’s available today — it’s the first thing to verify before building (see Task 3 open items).

5

Every dollar figure is an illustrative unit cost on a real volume

No provider cost or latency data exists in this dataset. The volumes driving the cost tiles above (101,162 stranded, 48,598 already-exhausted, Task 1’s 57/29/13 branch split) are real and validated; the $1.00–$3.50 per-call and $8-per-case figures are placeholders standing in for real vendor and ops numbers, and are the first thing to challenge or replace.

6

Persona IDV’s friction cost isn’t quantifiable from this data

A 10–20× volume increase into a document-and-selfie flow carries a real drop-off risk. This dataset is a verification-system log, not product funnel telemetry — it has no signal on how many people abandon at that step, so the friction cost above is named as a risk, not sized as a number.
